What I think the job is.

Good product leadership is about making thinking more visible.

When teams understand the why behind the problem they're solving, they get more excited about owning the how. When they don't, they just execute. And when the business can't see product as problem solvers, they start treating it as a delivery function.

A lot of my job is communicating, and building the loop where the team solves problems well and the business sees them as autonomous owners.

Where I think I add value.

A few quick responses to things that stood out in your brief.

On "what stays, what gets rebuilt, what plugs in natively, and what gets strangled out":

Removing things is the work most product teams underdo. I'd want my team looking at the data every quarter and being honest about what's earning its place and what isn't. I'd back them to make the call. Done this before at Thriva. Killed a couple of packages that weren't working, and the core product started performing properly once they were out of the way.

On PMs operating as "Product Builders, not backlog managers":

Agree that PMs should be builders. They get there when they've got the skill, the data, and the air cover. Skill and data can be taught. Air cover is my job. Simplyhealth has been a proper case study in doing this transition at pace and under budget constraints.

On conversion as a "shared discipline with Marketing":

My product background is in growth, with stints in marketing functions too. The best work happens when both sides are pointed at the same problem, and that's how I've set the digital squads up at Simplyhealth. The product is only worth as much as the number of people using it, which makes acquisition and experience the same conversation.
